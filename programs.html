Nine programs · Three stages

A nine-program pathway from first spoken sentences to confident, expressive Kannada, and a community that stays with you.

Each program has its own practical outcome. Learners may enrol in one or more programs, while higher programs require completion of the preceding foundation.

Meet Anand, the teacher, and Rahul, the traveller. Turn the page and follow their journey, one chapter at a time.

Program overview

Stage 1 teaches you to speak. Stage 2 teaches you to read and write. Stage 3 keeps you growing and keeps you company. Choose any program below to jump straight to it.

  1. Stage 1 · Speak (Programs 1–4) Spoken Kannada Foundations: from your first sentences to independent everyday Kannada, with no script to learn first. You can stop here if spoken Kannada is all you need.
  2. Stage 2 · Read & Write (Programs 5–8) Learn the script and written Kannada, so you can read books, follow films and keep practising on your own.
  3. Stage 3 · Belong (Program 9) Grow your words through immersion and performance, and join an alumni community that stays with you.

Program 1 · Kannada First Steps

What you will learn

Program 1 is about speaking for yourself. You learn only the sentences where you are the one doing it, but you learn them completely: how the word for “I” changes to mean my, mine, to me, for me, from me, with me, in me and on me, and how to say what you do. There is no script to learn and no rules to memorise. You copy the pattern, say it aloud, and it sticks.

  • Say what you do: “I go to the office.” “I come from home.”
  • Say what you give, take and do with others: “I give this book to him.” “I will take it from him.” “I will go with him.”
  • Say what is yours: “That is my book.” “That book is mine.”
  • The many forms of “I”: I, my, mine, to me, for me, because of me, from me, with me, in me, on me.
  • One everyday form covers both “I go” and “I will go”, so you can talk about now and soon with the same words.
  • A small starter set of everyday action words, such as go, come, give, take and eat, so you can build your first real sentences.

By the end, you can

  • Speak about yourself in simple, complete sentences.
  • Use “I” in all its forms: to me, for me, from me, with me, in me, on me.
  • Talk about what you do, where you go, what you give and take, and what is yours.
  • Start speaking from the first weeks, without needing the script.

Program 2 · Kannada in Context

What you will learn

Program 2 stretches everything from Program 1 in two ways. First, you learn new kinds of sentences: questions, “will not” sentences and requests. Second, you learn to use them for every person, not only “I”. You are still learning by patterns, not rule lists.

  • Ask questions: “Will you go?” “Where will you go?”, with the basic question words: who, what, where, when, how much.
  • Say what someone does not or will not do: “I will not go.” “He will not come.”
  • Make requests and give instructions, politely or to a friend: “Please go.” “Go!”
  • Talk about anyone, not just yourself: he, she, they, we, you, an elder, a group.
  • Use the same “to, for, from, with, in, on” word forms for everyone, not only for “I”.

By the end, you can

  • Ask and answer everyday questions.
  • Say what you and others will not do.
  • Make polite requests and friendly instructions.
  • Talk about any person (a man, a woman, a group or an elder) in all the situations from Program 1.
  • Introduce yourself, ask simple questions, describe basic actions, and manage simple everyday exchanges.

Program 3 · Kannada in Flow

What you will learn

Most courses stop at “I go” and “I went”. Program 3 teaches you to express time fully in real spoken Kannada: what is happening, what is finished, what you do habitually, and what you intend to do. You take everything from Programs 1 and 2 and learn to say it in the past, the present and the future, and as something still going on or already finished. After this program you can hold real conversations, though your collection of words is still small. Adding words is the next step.

  • Past, present and future: “I went.” “I go.” “I will go.”
  • Things happening right now: “I am going.” “She is reading.”
  • Things that are finished, and experiences: “I have gone.” “I have been to Mysuru.”
  • Habits and plans: “I go there every day.” “I am planning to go.”
  • All of this in every kind of sentence from Programs 1 and 2: saying, asking, saying “not”, and requesting.
  • All of this for every person, with all the “to, from, with” forms.

By the end, you can

  • Talk about yesterday, today and tomorrow.
  • Say what is going on now, and what is finished.
  • Hold simple conversations on many everyday topics, with the words you know so far.
  • Be ready to add more words, which is Program 4.

Program 4 · Word by Word

What you will learn

Program 4 is about words. By now you know how sentences are built; here you load them with everyday vocabulary, theme by theme. Many learners are happy to stop here if speaking is all they need. Those who want to read and write continue to Stage 2.

  • Everyday action words, naming words and describing words: what people do, the things around them, and how things are, such as big, small, tasty, difficult.
  • Feelings and ideas, not only objects: happy, afraid, honest, important.
  • Little words that join and place things: “and”, “with”, “after”, “near”, “quickly”.
  • Themes: numbers (1 to 100, first, second, third, and big numbers), time, dates, family, body parts, colours, directions, weather, food, shopping and emergency phrases.
  • Common phrases that go together naturally, with small practical tasks to use them.
  • Practical phrases for real Karnataka situations: cabs, shops, restaurants, apartments and workplaces.

By the end, you can

  • Use a wide, practical vocabulary across everyday life.
  • Speak Kannada on many subjects, not only simple ones.
  • Stop here if spoken Kannada is all you need, or move on to reading and writing.

Program 5 · Meet the Letters

What you will learn

Program 5 teaches only the script. There is nothing new to say and nothing new to learn about sentences. You simply learn to read and write what you can already say. Because you speak first, the letters feel familiar rather than overwhelming.

  • The letters and their sounds, one by one.
  • How letters join to make words.
  • Reading words you already speak: signs, labels and simple conversations.
  • Writing basic words and short sentences.
  • The sentences from Programs 1 to 4, now read and written.

By the end, you can

  • Read and write the Kannada script.
  • Read simple words, signs and labels.
  • Write basic words and sentences you already speak.
  • Be ready for written Kannada in the next programs.

Program 6 · Kannada with Clarity

What you will learn

Program 6 opens the box. You have been speaking in patterns. Now you read and write the same sentences properly, and learn why they look the way they do. The focus is on written Kannada.

  • How written sentences are put together properly.
  • How a word changes when you say to, from, in, with or of, and how to build those forms yourself.
  • The “base word” of an action (like “hogu”, meaning “go”), and how the endings added to it tell you who is doing it and when.
  • Reading short passages and writing simple messages.
  • Everything from Programs 1 to 4, revisited with the reasons behind it.

By the end, you can

  • Build correct written forms of words and actions yourself, instead of memorising each one.
  • Read and write more accurately.
  • Read short passages and write simple messages and paragraphs.
  • Keep speaking naturally while you do it.

Program 7 · Kannada on the Page

What you will learn

Program 7 takes what Program 3 taught you to say about time and puts it on paper. You learn the careful, complete way that books, messages and newspapers say it.

  • Things happening now, things finished, and experiences, all in writing.
  • How casual talk and formal writing say these things a little differently.
  • Reading and writing sentences that use them.
  • Reading short passages and writing short messages about what happened and what will happen.

By the end, you can

  • Tell the difference in meaning between “I eat”, “I am eating” and “I have eaten”, and write each correctly.
  • Read sentences like these in books and messages.
  • Use the right form for casual and for formal writing.

Program 8 · Rich Conversations

What you will learn

By now your toolkit is strong, but real Kannada also has long sentences, formal writing and small linking words. Program 8 fills those gaps, through conversations, explanations, stories, letters, essays and passages like those in school textbooks. It is for speaking as well as writing.

  • Joining ideas into one sentence: “The man who came yesterday is my uncle.” “Because it rained, I stayed home.”
  • Small linking words that never change, like and, but, so and also.
  • How sounds blend when words meet: “mane” + “inda” becomes “maneyinda” (from the house).
  • Two words fused into one: “tande” (father) + “taayi” (mother) becomes “tandetaayi” (father and mother).
  • Saying the same thing in a different way, including from everyday speech to formal writing.

By the end, you can

  • Understand and make longer, richer sentences in speech and in writing.
  • Use linking words, blended sounds and fused words correctly.
  • Rewrite sentences in other ways.
  • Speak and write naturally, without big gaps in the basics.

Program 9 · Unstoppable

What you will learn

Program 9 is where words grow on their own. You meet real Kannada in newspapers, films and conversations, and you use it in role plays, acting and discussion. These activities strengthen the words you have learned and bring you new ones, along with sayings and proverbs, in context.

  • Real Kannada: newspapers and articles, news clips, film scenes, interviews and stories.
  • Doing: roleplay, acting, discussions, talking about your own experiences, reviews, reflections and short pieces of writing.
  • New words, sayings and proverbs, picked up from context.
  • Using what you have learned, again and again, until it feels effortless.

By the end, you can

  • Understand new words from the way they are used.
  • Take part in roleplays, acting and discussions.
  • Speak about real-life subjects with more variety, confidence and range, in speaking and in writing.
  • Keep your vocabulary growing, through real-world engagement.

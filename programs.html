Program overview

Stage 1 teaches you to speak. Stage 2 teaches you to read, write, and understand grammar. Stage 3 keeps you growing — and keeps you company. Choose any program below to jump straight to it.

  1. Stage 1 · Speak (Programs 1–4) Spoken Kannada Foundations — from your first sentences to independent everyday Kannada, with no script to learn first. You can stop here if spoken Kannada is all you need.

Program 1 · Kannada First Steps

What you will learn

Program 1 is about speaking for yourself. You learn only the sentences where you are the one doing it, but you learn them completely: how the word for “I” changes to mean my, mine, to me, for me, from me, with me, in me and on me, and how to say what you do. There is no script to learn. You copy the pattern, say it aloud, and it sticks.

  • Say what you do: “I go to the office.” “I come from home.”
  • Say what you give, take and do with others: “I give this book to him.” “I will take it from him.” “I will go with him.”
  • Say what is yours: “That is my book.” “That book is mine.”
  • Use "I" in all its forms — I, my, mine, to me, for me, because of me, from me, with me, in me, and on me.
  • Talk about now and soon with the same words — one everyday form covers both "I go" and "I will go."
  • Build your first real sentences from a starter set of action words — go, come, give, take, and eat.
  • Start speaking from the first weeks — with no script to learn.

Program 2 · Kannada in Context

What you will learn

Program 2 stretches everything from Program 1 in two ways. First, you learn new kinds of sentences: questions, "will not" sentences, and requests. Second, you learn to use them for every person, not only "I". You are still learning by patterns, not rule lists.

  • Ask questions — "Will you go?" "Where will you go?" — with the basic question words: who, what, where, when, and how much.
  • Say what someone does not or will not do — "I will not go." "He will not come."
  • Make requests and give instructions, politely or to a friend — "Please go." "Go!"
  • Talk about anyone, not just yourself — he, she, they, we, you, an elder, or a group.
  • Use the same "to, for, from, with, in, and on" word forms for everyone — not only for "I".
  • Use everything from Program 1 in these new sentences — introduce yourself, ask simple questions, describe basic actions, and manage simple everyday exchanges.

Program 3 · Kannada in Flow

What you will learn

Most courses stop at "I go" and "I went". Program 3 teaches you to express time fully in real spoken Kannada: what is happening, what is finished, what you do habitually, and what you intend to do. You take everything from Programs 1 and 2 and learn to say it in the past, the present, and the future — as something still going on, or already finished.

  • Talk about the past, present, and future — "I went." "I go." "I will go."
  • Say what is happening right now — "I am going." "She is reading."
  • Say what is finished, and share experiences — "I have gone." "I have been to Mysuru."
  • Talk about habits and plans — "I go there every day." "I am planning to go."
  • Use all of this in every kind of sentence from Programs 1 and 2 — statements, questions, "will not" sentences, and requests.
  • Use all of this for every person — with all the "to, from, with" word forms.
  • Hold simple conversations on many everyday topics with the words you know so far — then add more words in Program 4.

Program 4 · Word by Word

What you will learn

Program 4 is about words. By now you know how sentences are built; here you load them with everyday vocabulary, theme by theme. Many learners are happy to stop here if speaking is all they need. Those who want to read and write continue to Stage 2.

  • Everyday action words, naming words, and describing words — what people do, the things around them, and how things are: big, small, tasty, difficult.
  • Feelings and ideas, not only objects — happy, afraid, honest, important.
  • Little words that join and place things — "and," "with," "after," "near," "quickly."
  • Everyday themes — numbers (1 to 100, first, second, third, and big numbers), time, dates, family, body parts, colours, directions, weather, food, shopping, and emergency phrases.
  • Common phrases that go together naturally — with small practical tasks to use them.
  • Practical phrases for real Karnataka situations — cabs, shops, restaurants, apartments, and workplaces.
  • A wide, practical vocabulary across everyday life — so you can speak Kannada on many subjects, not only simple ones.

Program 5 · Meet the Letters

What you will learn

Program 5 teaches only the script. There is nothing new to say and nothing new to learn about sentences. You simply learn to read and write the words you can already say. Because you speak first, the letters feel familiar rather than overwhelming.

  • The letters and their sounds, one by one.
  • How letters join to make words.
  • Reading words you already speak: signs, labels and simple conversations.
  • Writing basic words and short sentences.
  • Be ready to read and write sentences in the next programs.

Program 6 · Kannada with Clarity

What you will learn

Program 6 opens the box. You have been speaking in patterns; now you read and write the same sentences properly, and learn why they look the way they do. The focus is on written Kannada.

  • How written sentences are put together properly.
  • How a word changes when you say to, from, in, with or of, and how to build those forms yourself.
  • The “base word” of an action (like “hogu”, meaning “go”), and how the endings added to it tell you who is doing it and when.
  • Reading short passages and writing simple messages.
  • Revisit everything from Programs 1 & 2 — now by applying written kannada grammar.
  • Build correct written forms of words and actions.

Program 8 · Rich Conversations

What you will learn

By now your toolkit is strong, but real Kannada also has long sentences, formal writing and small linking words. Program 8 fills those gaps, through conversations, explanations, stories, letters, essays and passages like those in school textbooks.

  • Joining ideas into one sentence: “The man who came yesterday is my uncle.” “Because it rained, I stayed home.”
  • Small linking words that never change — and and, but, so and also.
  • How sounds blend when words meet: “mane” + “inda” becomes “maneyinda” (from the house).
  • Two words fused into one: “tande” (father) + “taayi” (mother) becomes “tandetaayi” (father and mother).
  • Understand and build longer, richer sentences — in speech and in writing.

Program 9

Unstoppable Your Stage is Ready – Perform

Grow your words by living in Kannada — through the alumni community.

As part of the alumni, you take part in regular activities that keep your Kannada alive: roleplays, acting, discussions, movie nights, news discussions, and speaking circles. You meet real Kannada in newspapers, films, and conversations, and you use it again and again until it feels effortless.

Alumni activities

  • Join monthly role-play sessions.
  • Watch and discuss Kannada movies and clips.
  • Enact short scripts.
  • Join speaking circles.
  • Share real-life Kannada experiences.
  • Take part in vocabulary challenges.
  • Practise with newer learners.
  • Stay connected to the teacher and the community.

What you gain

  • New words, sayings, and proverbs, picked up from context.
  • Confidence in roleplays, acting, and discussions.
  • More range and variety in speaking and writing about real life.
  • A vocabulary that keeps growing through real-world engagement.
  • Belonging, fun, and friendship with learners from other batches.
